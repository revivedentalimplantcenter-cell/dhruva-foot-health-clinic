Monsoon Foot Care for People With Diabetes: Staying Safe in Wet Weather

14 October 2026 · 4 min read

తెలుగులో చదవండి

Rainy weather is hard on the feet, and harder still on diabetic feet. Dampness softens the skin, wet footwear invites fungal infections, and puddles hide sharp objects that can injure feet that have lost feeling. Good diabetic foot care during the monsoon is mostly about one thing: keeping the feet clean and dry.

Why wet feet are risky in diabetes

When feet stay wet for long, the skin between the toes becomes soft and breaks down easily — the perfect entry point for fungal infections and bacterial infections alike. In people with diabetic neuropathy, reduced sensation means a soaked, rubbed-raw patch of skin can go unnoticed for days. With poor circulation added to the mix, a minor skin breakdown can develop into a diabetic foot ulcer that heals slowly and needs professional wound care.

Keeping feet dry through the rains

  • Dry thoroughly after every wetting. When your feet get wet, wash them with clean water, then dry gently but completely — especially between the toes. Softening moisture left between the toes is where most problems start.
  • Do not apply moisturiser between the toes. Moisturise the rest of the foot if the skin is dry, but the spaces between the toes must be kept dry, not moist.
  • Carry a spare pair of socks. Change into dry socks if yours get damp during the day, and let shoes dry fully before wearing them again.
  • Check for fungal infections between toes. Itching, white soggy skin, peeling or a bad smell between the toes are signs of a fungal infection — get it treated early rather than waiting for it to spread or break the skin.

Choosing safe monsoon footwear

  • Wear waterproof or quick-drying footwear — rubber or microfibre sandals and shoes that shed water and dry fast are better than leather or cloth shoes that stay soggy.
  • Never walk barefoot through puddles or wet ground — wet mud and water hide nails, glass, stones and thorns. Insensitive feet can be cut without you feeling it.
  • Check the insides of your shoes before wearing them — rain washes grit, small stones and even insects into footwear left outside.
  • Break in new rainy-season footwear gradually so it does not rub blisters into softened, rain-wet skin.

Watch for these warning signs

Inspect your feet every evening during the monsoon. Seek medical care promptly if you notice any break in the skin that is not healing, spreading redness or swelling, a foul smell, blisters, or new numbness or pain. At a foot clinic in Karimnagar such as ours, most early-stage problems can be managed in a routine visit — but infected wounds need professional attention without delay.
