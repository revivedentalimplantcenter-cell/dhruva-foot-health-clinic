Moisturising Your Feet Safely: A Diabetic Foot Care Guide for Karimnagar

23 October 2026 · 3 min read

తెలుగులో చదవండి

Good diabetic foot care in Karimnagar clinics and homes starts with the skin. Diabetes often leaves the feet unusually dry — and dry skin cracks, callus builds up, and those cracks can become entry points for infection. Daily moisturising is one of the simplest habits that protects diabetic feet, but it has to be done the right way.

Why diabetic feet get so dry

Diabetic neuropathy can damage the nerves that control sweating in the feet. When the feet no longer sweat normally, the skin loses its natural moisture and becomes dry, flaky and stiff. This dryness makes the skin less elastic, so it splits under the pressure of walking — especially on the heels and the balls of the feet. Keeping the skin soft and supple through regular moisturising is a core part of wound care and prevention.

Where to apply moisturiser — and where not to

Apply a generous layer of cream over the tops and soles of the feet, the heels and the backs of the ankles. Massage it in gently so it is absorbed rather than sitting on the surface. There is one strict rule every foot clinic in Karimnagar will repeat: never apply moisturiser between the toes. The skin between the toes is already moist, and extra cream there traps dampness — creating exactly the warm, wet conditions in which fungal infections grow.

Choosing the right moisturiser

  • Plain, fragrance-free creams or ointments work best — fragrances and dyes can irritate fragile skin.
  • Creams with urea or lanolin are good choices for thick, dry skin; they draw moisture in and keep it there.
  • Avoid alcohol-based lotions — they feel light but dry the skin out further.
  • Avoid strongly scented or medicated creams unless a doctor has advised them for you.
  • Do not use petroleum jelly between the toes, and never apply any product inside an open crack or wound — that needs professional wound care.

When and how to moisturise

The best time is right after washing and drying your feet — warm, clean, fully dry skin absorbs cream best. Make sure the spaces between the toes are completely dry first. Many people find moisturising before bed works well, as the cream has hours to work overnight. If your feet are very dry, twice a day is reasonable. Always pair moisturising with your daily foot check: soft skin still needs inspecting for cuts, blisters or redness.

What to do about deep cracks

Regular moisturising prevents most cracking, but if a heel or skin area has already split deeply, is bleeding, or shows redness around the crack, do not try to treat it with cream or cut the hard skin yourself. Deep cracks can turn into foot ulcers quickly in diabetes. Keep the area clean, protect it, and have it assessed by a specialist — safe debridement of hard skin is a job for trained hands, not for blades or pumice stones at home.

This article is for general information only and is not a substitute for medical advice. If you have a foot wound or worrying symptoms, please consult a doctor.
