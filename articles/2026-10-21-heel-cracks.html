Cracked Heels and Diabetes: Prevention and Care

21 October 2026 · 3 min read

తెలుగులో చదవండి

Cracked heels may look like a cosmetic problem, but for people with diabetes they are a genuine health risk. Deep cracks in dry skin can become an entry point for infection — and a wound on the foot of a person with diabetes can take far longer to heal. Good diabetic foot care in Karimnagar means treating heel cracks seriously, not just covering them with footwear.

Why diabetes makes heel cracks more likely

High blood sugar damages nerves over time, including the small nerves that control sweating. When the feet sweat less, the skin — especially on the heels — becomes dry and loses its elasticity. Walking, standing for long periods, and wearing open-backed footwear then let the dry skin thicken into callus, which cracks under pressure. Reduced blood flow to the feet makes it harder for these cracks to close on their own.

Preventing cracked heels: the daily routine

  • Moisturise every day — apply a simple, unscented moisturiser or petroleum jelly to the heels and soles after washing, while the skin is still slightly damp. Never apply moisturiser between the toes; the trapped dampness can soften the skin there and invite fungal infection.
  • Wear closed, supportive footwear — open-backed sandals let the heel expand sideways and encourage cracking; closed shoes with a cushioned sole protect the skin and spread pressure more evenly.
  • Check your heels when you do your daily foot check — look for dryness, thickened skin, or the first fine lines before they deepen into fissures.
  • Stay hydrated and keep blood sugar in range — both affect how well the skin holds moisture and how quickly small breaks heal.
  • Avoid soaking feet for long periods — a brief wash is fine, but prolonged soaking dries the skin out further.

What to do if cracks have already formed

  • Continue daily moisturising and switch to closed footwear to stop the cracks deepening.
  • Gently reduce thickened callus with a pumice stone on damp skin — never use blades, razors or sharp instruments, and stop if it hurts or bleeds.
  • Watch the cracks closely: any bleeding, redness spreading from a crack, swelling, warmth or discharge means the crack has become infected or ulcerated and needs professional care at a foot clinic in Karimnagar.
  • Do not ignore a deep crack that is not improving after a week of care — cracks that reach deeper layers of skin are essentially open wounds.

When heel cracks become a foot ulcer risk

A deep fissure in the heel is an open wound. In a person with diabetic neuropathy, it may not hurt, so it can go unnoticed and become infected. If a crack is bleeding, has a hard rim of callus around it, or shows any sign of infection, it should be assessed the way any foot wound would be — this is exactly the kind of problem the wound care team at Dhruva Foot Health Clinic handles every day. Early treatment of a cracking heel is always simpler than treating a diabetic foot ulcer that grew from one.
