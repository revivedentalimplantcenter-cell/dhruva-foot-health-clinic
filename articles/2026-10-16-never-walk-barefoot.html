Why People With Diabetes Should Never Walk Barefoot

16 October 2026 · 4 min read

తెలుగులో చదవండి

Walking barefoot is one of the most common causes of diabetic foot ulcers — and one of the easiest risks to remove. Good diabetic foot care in Karimnagar starts with a simple habit: keep your feet covered at all times, indoors and outdoors. Here is why this rule matters so much for people with diabetes.

You may not feel the injury

Diabetic neuropathy dulls sensation in the feet over time. A person with reduced feeling can step on a thorn, a hot kitchen floor, broken glass or a sharp pebble and never notice the injury. Because there is no pain to warn them, the wound goes uncleaned and untreated for hours or days. Anyone beginning diabetic neuropathy treatment should treat unnoticed injuries as the number one risk to avoid.

Small wounds heal slowly in diabetes

Even when an injury is spotted, poor circulation means less blood — and fewer infection-fighting cells — reaches the foot. High blood sugar further slows healing. A puncture or blister that would heal in days for someone without diabetes can linger for weeks, giving bacteria time to cause an infection that may need professional diabetic foot ulcer treatment.

Hot surfaces and floors can burn without warning

Bathroom tiles heated by a geyser, a sun-baked courtyard, or sand outdoors can be hot enough to burn skin. Feet with numbness cannot sense the heat, so a burn can develop silently. Keeping footwear on is the simplest form of protection against these hidden injuries.

Safe habits instead of bare feet

  • Wear soft, closed footwear indoors — a pair of comfortable house slippers or chappals kept only for indoor use.
  • Choose outdoor footwear that covers the toes and sole, with a sturdy base that blocks sharp objects.
  • Before stepping out, shake out your footwear and check the inside for stones or rough seams.
  • After any accidental contact with a sharp object or hot surface, inspect both feet the same day and clean any break in the skin.
  • If you must remove footwear — for example, in a place of worship — walk only on clean, cool surfaces and put footwear back on immediately.
  • See a foot ulcer doctor in Karimnagar promptly if you find a cut, puncture or blister you cannot explain.

When a small injury needs a specialist

Contact a foot clinic in Karimnagar without delay if you notice redness, swelling, warmth, discharge, a sore that is not improving within a day or two, or any new numbness or burning.
