Inspecting the Inside of Your Shoes: Everyday Diabetic Foot Care in Karimnagar

25 October 2026 · 4 min read

తెలుగులో చదవండి

Before you put on your shoes each morning, take thirty seconds to check what is inside them. For people with diabetes, this small habit is an important part of diabetic foot care in Karimnagar — because a pebble, a folded sock or a rough seam can injure the foot without ever being felt, and checking first can prevent a wound that takes weeks to heal.

Why a small object can cause a serious problem

Diabetes can damage the nerves in the feet, a condition known as diabetic neuropathy. When feeling is reduced, the foot does not warn you about pressure, rubbing or a sharp object inside the shoe. A person without diabetes would feel a stone at once and stop; a person with neuropathy may walk on it all day.

That repeated pressure can first cause redness or a blister. Because diabetes also slows healing and reduces blood flow to the feet, a blister can break down into an open sore — a diabetic foot ulcer — which may need professional diabetic foot ulcer treatment. This is why diabetic neuropathy treatment and regular foot checks go hand in hand with daily habits at home.

What to look for inside your shoes

  • Loose objects — small stones, sand or anything that fell in while the shoes were off.
  • Folded or bunched material — a sock that has slipped down, or an insole curled up at the edge.
  • Rough seams and torn lining — loose stitching, or lining worn thin and rough.
  • Worn or uneven insoles — insoles thinned in one place can create pressure points, which over time contribute to corns, callus and heel pain.
  • Sharp points — a nail or staple working through the sole or side of the shoe.
  • Dampness — shoes still wet inside from rain or sweat, which soften the skin and make blisters more likely.

The 30-second inspection routine

  • Shake them out. Hold each shoe upside down, shake it firmly, then tap the heel.
  • Look inside. Hold the shoe up to the light and look for objects, tears or loose stitching.
  • Feel with your hand. Run your whole hand slowly over the inside of the shoe, including the toe area, the seams and under the insole if it lifts out. Your hand can feel what a numb foot cannot.
  • Flatten the insole. Push a curled or bunched insole flat, and replace insoles that are worn thin or cracked.
  • Check your socks too. Make sure socks are smooth, without holes or thick seams pressing on the toes.

If you find something — or find a sore

If you find an object, remove it and check the shoe again before wearing it. If the shoe itself is damaged — a torn lining, a loose nail or a badly worn insole — repair it or replace it rather than continuing to wear it.

After removing your shoes, look at your feet as well. If you see redness, a blister, a cut or a sore — even a small one — do not ignore it, and do not try to treat a deep or spreading wound at home.
