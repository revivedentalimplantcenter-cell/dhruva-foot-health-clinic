The 5-Minute Daily Foot Check Every Person With Diabetes Should Do

13 October 2026 · 4 min read

Diabetes can damage the nerves in your feet — a condition called neuropathy — which means a cut, blister or burn may not hurt the way it should. Many serious foot ulcers begin as small injuries that went unnoticed for days. A short daily check is the simplest way to catch problems early, when they are easiest to treat.

How to do the check

Pick a time you will remember — many people do it while getting dressed in the morning or before bed. Sit somewhere with good light and look at the whole of each foot, including:

  • The soles and heels — use a hand mirror, or ask a family member, if you cannot see them easily.
  • Between the toes — moisture and fungal infections often start here.
  • The toenails — look for thickening, discolouration or ingrown edges.

What to look for

  • Cuts, scratches, blisters or sores — even small ones.
  • Redness, warmth or swelling in any area.
  • New corns, callus or cracked skin, especially on the heels.
  • Changes in skin colour or temperature between the two feet.
  • Any discharge or unusual smell from a wound.

What to do if you find something

Clean a minor cut gently, cover it with a clean dressing and watch it closely. Do not try to cut away corns or callus yourself, and do not apply strong antiseptics or home remedies to an open wound. If a sore shows redness spreading around it, swelling, discharge, or does not start improving within a day or two, see a foot specialist promptly — early treatment prevents most complications.

Make it a habit

Keep a small mirror and a torch by your chair or bed so there is no excuse to skip a day. People who check daily consistently spot trouble weeks earlier than those who don’t — and with diabetic foot problems, those weeks matter.

This article is for general information only and is not a substitute for medical advice. If you have a foot wound or worrying symptoms, please consult a doctor.
