What Happens During a Professional Foot Assessment: Diabetic Foot Care in Karimnagar

1 November 2026 · 4 min read

Many people with diabetes put off a foot check because they are not sure what it involves. A professional foot assessment is a simple, structured examination of your feet. It helps a foot clinic in Karimnagar find problems early, before they become wounds or infections, and it gives you a clear plan for looking after your feet at home. Here is what usually happens, step by step.

Your medical history comes first

The assessment usually starts with a conversation. The doctor will ask how long you have had diabetes, how your blood sugar has been, and whether you have had any foot wounds, ulcers, or infections before. You will also be asked about numbness, tingling, burning, or pain in your feet, and about your footwear and daily routine. Bringing a list of your medicines helps the doctor build a complete picture.

Diabetic foot care in Karimnagar: skin, nails and shape

Next, both feet are examined closely. The doctor looks at the skin for cuts, blisters, redness, swelling, cracks, corns, and callus, and checks the toenails for thickening, fungal changes, or ingrown edges. The shape of the feet and toes is also noted, since changes in shape can shift pressure to areas that are more likely to break down. An existing wound is examined for size, depth, and signs of infection, and measured so healing can be tracked.

Feeling and circulation tests

Two areas matter most in diabetic foot assessment: feeling and blood flow. Feeling is often tested with a thin filament pressed gently against different points on the sole, and sometimes with a tuning fork to check vibration sense. These simple tests show whether diabetic neuropathy treatment is needed and how much protective feeling has been lost. Circulation is checked by feeling the pulses in the feet, looking at skin colour and temperature, and asking about pain while walking. If circulation looks reduced, further tests may follow.

Footwear, pressure and your daily routine

Your shoes are part of the assessment too. The doctor may look at the fit, the wear pattern on the soles, and whether the shoes press on toes or heels. Pressure points on the soles — especially under the ball of the foot — are checked, since these are common sites for ulcers in people with diabetes. You will also be asked about your daily foot-care routine: how you wash and dry your feet, how you trim your nails, and whether you ever walk barefoot.

What happens after the assessment

At the end, the doctor will explain the findings in plain language and agree on a plan with you. For many people, that plan is simple: a daily foot-check routine, advice on footwear, and a date for the next review. If a wound, infection, or circulation problem is found, wound care in Karimnagar or other treatment may be arranged, and you may be referred to a foot ulcer doctor in Karimnagar for specialist care. Regular reviews, even when your feet feel fine, catch problems while they are still easy to treat.
