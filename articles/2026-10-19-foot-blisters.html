Foot Blisters and Diabetes: Do's and Don'ts Before They Turn Into Ulcers

19 October 2026 · 4 min read

తెలుగులో చదవండి

A blister may look like a small problem, but for a person with diabetes it is often the first step towards a diabetic foot ulcer. Repeated rubbing from shoes or heat from a hot surface can raise a blister in hours, and if it breaks, infection follows quickly in feet with poor circulation. Learning how to prevent blisters — and how to respond when one appears — is a key part of diabetic foot ulcer treatment and prevention at our foot clinic in Karimnagar.

Why blisters are dangerous in diabetes

Three factors make blisters far more serious when you have diabetes:

  • Reduced feeling (neuropathy) — you may not notice the rubbing that causes the blister, so the pressure continues.
  • Poor circulation — less blood reaches the feet, so a burst blister heals slowly and infection risk is high.
  • Delayed healing — what stays a harmless blister for a week in a healthy person can become an open, infected ulcer in a person with diabetes.

This is why doctors treat every foot blister in a diabetic patient as a warning sign, not a minor irritation.

Preventing blisters: the do's

  • Do check the inside of every shoe before wearing it — run your hand inside to find rough seams, small stones or worn linings that rub.
  • Do break in new shoes slowly, wearing them for short periods at first and checking the feet afterwards for red spots.
  • Do wear clean, well-fitting socks every day; change them if they become damp, as moisture softens the skin and blisters form faster.
  • Do keep feet moisturised (avoiding between the toes) so the skin stays supple and resists friction.
  • Do protect your feet from hot water and hot surfaces — test bath water with your elbow, since reduced feeling can lead to burns that blister.

If a blister appears: the don'ts

  • Don't burst or peel the blister yourself. The roof of the blister protects the raw skin underneath from infection.
  • Don't ignore it. Cover it with a clean, dry dressing, stop the rubbing that caused it, and inspect the foot daily.
  • Don't keep walking on it. Relieve pressure by changing to softer footwear and reducing time on your feet.
  • Don't apply home remedies or strong antiseptics on broken skin without guidance — some can damage healing tissue.
  • Don't delay getting it checked. A blister that is filling with pus, spreading redness around it, swelling, or pain that increases needs prompt wound care — ideally at a clinic with experience in diabetic foot ulcer treatment like Dhruva Foot Health Clinic.

When a blister needs a foot specialist

Seek care without delay if the blister bursts and does not start improving within a day or two, if redness spreads beyond the blister's edge, if there is swelling or discharge, or if you have a fever. At our foot clinic in Karimnagar, a foot ulcer doctor assesses the wound, cleans it safely, protects it from pressure (offloading), and treats any early infection — steps that stop a simple blister from becoming a deep ulcer.
