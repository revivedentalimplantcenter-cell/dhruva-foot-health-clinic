Corns and Callus Treatment for Diabetic Feet: What They Mean and When to Get Help

26 October 2026 · 4 min read

తెలుగులో చదవండి

Corns and callus are areas of thickened skin that form where the foot is repeatedly pressed or rubbed. They are the skin's way of protecting itself, but for a person with diabetes they deserve attention rather than being ignored. Understanding corns and callus treatment begins with recognising what this thickened skin is signalling: that one part of the foot is carrying too much pressure.

What corns and callus are

Although they are often mentioned together, corns and callus are not quite the same:

  • Corns — small, well-defined areas of hard skin, often on the tops or sides of the toes. They may have a painful centre and hurt when pressed.
  • Callus — larger, flatter areas of thickened skin, most often on the soles of the feet or the heels. Callus is usually less painful, but it can still crack or break down.

Both are usually caused by repeated pressure or friction: tight or loose shoes, a rubbing seam, a change in foot shape, or the way weight falls on the foot. They are not infections, and they are not warts, although they can look similar.

Why they matter more in diabetes

For most people, a corn or callus is mainly uncomfortable. In diabetes, two complications change the picture: reduced feeling from diabetic neuropathy, so pressure and pain go unnoticed, and reduced blood circulation, so any crack heals slowly and is more open to infection.

  • Thick skin can hide pressure damage underneath it.
  • A crack in callus can become an open wound.
  • An open wound on a diabetic foot can develop into a foot ulcer, which is why diabetic foot ulcer treatment often starts with something as ordinary-looking as callus.
  • Pain may be absent even when damage is present, so a visual foot check matters more than waiting for discomfort.

That is why diabetic foot care in Karimnagar treats corns and callus as warning signs, not cosmetic problems.

Safe care and corns and callus treatment

Safe care focuses on reducing pressure and protecting the skin, rather than removing thick skin aggressively:

  • Do not cut, shave or dig at a corn or callus yourself, and do not use medicated corn plasters or strong acids without professional advice — these can burn surrounding skin and create a wound, especially in diabetes.
  • After washing, the skin can be smoothed very gently, but stop if the skin becomes red, sore or broken.
  • Moisturise the feet daily to keep the skin supple, avoiding applying moisturiser between the toes, where trapped moisture encourages fungal infection.
  • Wear well-fitting, cushioned footwear that does not squeeze the toes or rub one spot, and inspect the insides of shoes before wearing them.
  • A foot specialist can safely reduce thickened skin, identify the pressure point causing it, and advise on footwear or padding that spreads pressure more evenly.

When to get help promptly

Seek care at a foot clinic in Karimnagar promptly if a corn or callus becomes painful, red, swollen or warm, or if the skin cracks, bleeds, discharges fluid, or changes colour. At Dhruva Foot Health Clinic, foot assessments look at pressure points, circulation and feeling together.
